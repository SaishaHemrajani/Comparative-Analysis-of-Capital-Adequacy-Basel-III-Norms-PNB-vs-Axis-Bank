A five-year comparative study of Punjab National Bank and Axis Bank (FY2021–22 to FY2025–26), analysing capital adequacy ratios, CET1, Tier I &amp; II capital, Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA), leverage and regulatory capital requirements under RBI's Basel III framework.

# PNB vs Axis Bank – Comparative Analysis of Capital Adequacy & Basel III Norms

## Project Overview

This project presents a five-year comparative analysis of **Punjab National Bank (PNB)** and **Axis Bank**, examining their capital adequacy positions, regulatory capital structure and financial resilience under the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) Basel III framework.

The study evaluates key banking indicators from FY2021–22 to FY2025–26 to understand the differences in capital strength, loss-absorption capacity, leverage and risk-weighted asset growth between a public sector bank and a private sector bank.

## Objectives

- Compare the capital adequacy positions of PNB and Axis Bank over five financial years.
- Evaluate the composition and quality of regulatory capital.
- Analyse the trends in Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1), Additional Tier 1 (AT1), Tier 1, Tier 2 and Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio (CRAR).
- Examine Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA), leverage ratios and regulatory capital growth.
- Understand RBI's Basel III capital adequacy requirements and their implications for banking-sector stability.

## Key Areas of Analysis

**1. Capital Adequacy & Regulatory Capital**
- Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio (CRAR)
- Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1)
- Additional Tier 1 (AT1)
- Tier 1 and Tier 2 Capital
- Total Regulatory Capital

**2. Risk-Weighted Assets & Leverage**
- Five-year RWA trends
- Year-on-year RWA growth
- Leverage ratio comparison
- Relationship between risk exposure and regulatory capital requirements

**3. Basel Framework & RBI Regulations**
- Evolution of Basel I, Basel II, Basel 2.5 and Basel III
- RBI's minimum capital adequacy requirements
- Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB)
- Regulatory capital classification and risk-weighting methodology

## Key Findings

- **PNB:** CRAR improved from 14.50% in FY2021–22 to 17.74% in FY2025–26, indicating an improvement in its overall capital adequacy position.
- **Axis Bank:** CRAR declined from 18.54% to 16.42% over the same period, although it maintained a higher CET1 ratio than PNB throughout the period.
- **Capital Composition:** PNB recorded a higher Tier 1 ratio of 15.15% compared with Axis Bank's 14.78% in FY2025–26.
- **Leverage:** Axis Bank maintained a higher leverage ratio of 9.03% compared with PNB's 6.41% in FY2025–26.
- Both banks maintained CRAR levels above the 11.5% minimum requirement, including the Capital Conservation Buffer, as presented in the study.

## Conclusion

The comparative study highlights the importance of regulatory capital quality, capital adequacy and leverage in assessing banking-sector resilience. While PNB demonstrated an improvement in its overall CRAR over the five-year period, Axis Bank maintained a stronger CET1 position and higher leverage ratio in FY2025–26.

The analysis demonstrates how differences in capital composition, risk-weighted assets and regulatory capital management influence the financial resilience of public and private sector banks.
